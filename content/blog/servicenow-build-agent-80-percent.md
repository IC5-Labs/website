With AI, building enterprise applications is more efficient than ever before. ServiceNow's Build Agent can create workflows, automate business logic, build interfaces, and perform other tasks that used to take engineers significant time.

It sounds like a revolution, and in many aspects, it is. However, the big question that artificial intelligence still cannot answer is this: **what do you want to build for your business?**

This is a problem that often separates the winners from the losers in digital transformation. It is no longer about the ability to develop software faster. The main problem lies in recognizing the customer issue that needs to be solved.

More and more companies find out that artificial intelligence allows for accelerating the development process, but it does not replace strategy, insights, and business judgment. The organizations that benefit the most from AI are not the ones who can build applications quickly. They are the ones who know *why* they are building them.

## AI Can Create a Feature, But It Can't Recognize a Customer's Problem

AI development frameworks excel in implementation. Tell them what to do and they will build a viable solution in the blink of an eye.

But there is one thing they can't do — decide if that is the *right* thing to do. They don't know which customer problems deserve attention, which features will increase retention, or which flows will add actual business value. These questions should be answered by customers.

Every successful product starts with a problem that people encounter often. And until that problem is validated, AI helps to create a product that hasn't been validated yet.

## The Most Costly Software is the Software That Nobody Uses

A lot of companies consider slow development their biggest challenge. The thing is, there is something even more costly: creating products that nobody wants.

Product failures almost never occur due to the inability of engineers to code. They occur due to the creation of products that are based on assumptions rather than facts.

That is why customer signals are more important than development speed. Prior to making AI-generated applications, businesses need to know:

- What their customers are having problems with
- Which requests are recurring
- Where users abandon workflows
- How competitors are doing a better job solving problems
- What business outcomes are the most valuable

Otherwise, fast development just speeds up wrong decisions being made.

## Customer Signals are the Real Product Requirements

A great product roadmap doesn't come out of a planning session — it comes from the behaviors of your customers. The best signals can be found in places you already have access to:

**Customer Interviews**

Real interactions tell you the context that data alone can't. Why do your customers behave the way they do?

**Product Analytics**

User data tells you where people are spending their time, where they struggle, and what features they completely ignore.

**Customer Support Interactions**

Support requests reveal the consistent pain points that customers take the time to report.

**Sales Input**

Salespeople hear objections, feature requests, and comparisons all day long. This input is often an indicator of unmet market demand.

**Market Trends**

Regulations, changes in industries, and shifting customer demands affect what problems become strategic priorities.

This data answers a question that AI cannot: *Why does this feature matter?*

### Faster Development Doesn't Replace Product Discovery

The biggest misunderstanding when it comes to AI is that it enables companies to skip the research, validation, and customer discovery phases.

On the contrary, AI makes all of these even more important. If apps can now be built in days, not months, this means that one can test assumptions sooner, get feedback faster, and make changes more often.

Learning should happen *faster* because of AI — not stop. The companies getting the most value from AI learn quickly and use rapid development for validating assumptions, not to skip the tough customer questions.

### Good Products Start with Asking Good Questions

Excellent product teams don't start their journey with a technical question. They ask themselves:

- Why do our customers face this problem?
- How frequent is this issue?
- How much business impact does it have?
- For which customer group is it most relevant?
- How will we know that we succeeded?

Only after that do they develop their strategy. When the problem is fully understood, AI becomes even more powerful — because it is now solving a real problem.

## AI Is Becoming a Commodity. Customer Insights Are Not.

With the rise of widely available AI development technologies, coding is becoming easy for everyone.

What becomes hard and valuable is having a deeper understanding of customers than your competitors do. The new competitive advantage is shifting from fast coding to smart decision-making.

Firms that consistently spot genuine customer problems will beat firms that only develop more features. AI technology will help every company develop software faster. Customer insights will help every company develop the *right* software.

## ServiceNow Build Agent Is Capable — But It Requires Human Direction

ServiceNow's Build Agent represents a genuine advancement in enterprise application development. It can automate large parts of the development process and dramatically increase developer productivity.

It is not, however, a substitute for product strategy. It requires humans to formulate business goals, understand customer needs, prioritize opportunities, and define what success looks like.

**In other words: it can implement a vision. It cannot create one.**
